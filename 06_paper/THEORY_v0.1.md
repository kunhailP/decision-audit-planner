# Theory v0.1 — statements and proofs for the 2027 design (2026-09-10)

Notation. Target query distribution P. Menu M of policies with population utilities μ_m = E_P[u_m(q)], u_m ∈ [0,1].
Audit stream q_1, q_2, … i.i.d. from P (or a uniform random permutation of a finite target set; the without-replacement
case is conservative for the variance bounds below). Looks t = 1..L at sizes T_1 < … < T_L. At look t the audited set
S_t = {q_1..q_{T_t}} is split into A_t = first ⌊T_t/2⌋ and B_t = the rest. A judge is a fixed measurable map
f: q ↦ (û_m(q))_{m∈M} (any AI predictor; possibly biased, adversarial, or correlated with a policy).

## Proposition A (what determines the PPI effective-sample-size gain)

Fix policies j, m and let D = u_j − u_m, D̂ = û_j − û_m, with population variances σ², σ̂² and correlation ρ.
Let n = |B| audited queries and N ≫ n unlabeled queries. The PPI++ estimator with tuning λ,
  μ̂(λ) = λ·mean_N(D̂) + mean_n(D − λD̂),
has variance Var(μ̂(λ)) = [σ² − 2λ ρ σ σ̂ + λ² σ̂²]/n + λ² σ̂²/N.
At λ* = ρσ/σ̂ (N→∞) this equals σ²(1−ρ²)/n, so the effective-sample-size gain relative to the human-only
estimator (variance σ²/n) is exactly 1/(1−ρ²). It depends on f only through ρ = corr(D, D̂): the pair-level
accuracy of f enters only insofar as it changes ρ.

Proof. Direct computation of the variance of a linear combination of the two independent sample means; minimize the
quadratic in λ. ∎

Remark (policy-correlated judge). Write û_m = u_m + b_m + e_m with a policy-specific bias b_m(q) and noise e_m.
Then D̂ = D + (b_j − b_m) + (e_j − e_m). A bias that is *common* to j and m cancels; a bias that differs between the
two policies — e.g. one concentrated on documents that only policy j retrieves — enters D̂ and lowers ρ whenever
Var(b_j − b_m) is not small relative to Var(D). This is the mechanism measured in §12.2 (identical-model judge:
paired bias −0.14, ρ 0.57 → 0.25).

## Proposition B′ (conditional validity of the split certificate, any judge)

Let 𝒢_t be the σ-field generated by A_t (train half) and by all decisions taken on it: policy parameters, the
candidate m̂_t, the adoption decision a_t ∈ {PPI, human-only}. Let the certificate at look t, for each competitor j ≠ m̂_t,
be U_{t,j} = μ̂_{t,j} + z_{α'} · ŝe_{t,j}, computed on B_t only, where
  (i) if a_t = human-only: μ̂_{t,j} = mean_B(D_j), ŝe from the sample sd of D_j on B_t;
  (ii) if a_t = PPI: μ̂_{t,j} as in Prop. A with λ cross-fitted inside B_t (λ for fold 1 estimated on fold 2 and
       vice versa) and clipped to [0,1]; mean_N(D̂_j) taken over target queries outside B_t;
and α' = α / (L · |M|(|M|−1)).

Claim. Conditionally on 𝒢_t, and for every fixed f, E[μ̂_{t,j} | 𝒢_t] = μ_j − μ_{m̂_t}, and U_{t,j} is a level-(1−α')
upper confidence bound for μ_j − μ_{m̂_t} up to the t-approximation of the sampling distribution of the mean.
Consequently, with ACT_t := {max_{j≠m̂_t} U_{t,j} ≤ ε},
   P( ∃t: ACT_t and μ_{best} − μ_{m̂_t} > ε ) ≤ α,
regardless of f, of the adoption rule, and of whether the ρ-based adoption score covers its target.

Proof. Given 𝒢_t, the queries in B_t are an i.i.d. sample from P independent of everything in 𝒢_t (they were not used
to form A_t's decisions). (i) is the usual t-bound. For (ii): within each fold, λ was estimated on the other fold and
is therefore 𝒢_t ∨ σ(other fold)-measurable and independent of the fold it is applied to; the queries used for
mean_N(D̂_j) lie outside B_t. Hence conditionally on λ, E[D_j − λD̂_j] = μ_j − μ_{m̂_t} − λ E[D̂_j] and
E[λ·mean_N(D̂_j)] = λE[D̂_j], so the estimator is conditionally unbiased for any f; its conditional variance is the
sum of the two independent terms in Prop. A, which ŝe estimates consistently. If the ACT at look t is wrong, then
for j* = argmax μ_j we have μ_{j*} − μ_{m̂_t} > ε ≥ U_{t,j*}, i.e. the bound (t, j*) failed. The candidate m̂_t is
𝒢_t-measurable, so the failing pair (t, j*) is one of the L·|M|(|M|−1) ordered pairs covered by the union bound over
all looks and all ordered pairs; each fails with conditional probability ≤ α'. ∎

Remarks. (1) The adoption rule and the ρ lower bound never enter the bound: a bad rule only changes *which* valid bound
(t or PPI) is used, hence only efficiency. (2) Clipping λ to [0,1] keeps the estimator unbiased (λ is fold-independent).
(3) The residual approximations are: normal/t approximation for the mean of bounded variables at n ≥ 5 per fold, and the
finite-population correction for sampling without replacement (which makes the bound conservative). (4) The v0.3
certificate violated the premise in two ways: the candidate was chosen on the same data used for the bound with only
|M|−1 comparisons corrected (winner's curse; measured 0.049 vs budget 0.020 in synthetic data), and LOO-cross-fitted
utilities are not independent across queries.

## Proposition C (band localisation of decision-relevant judgments)

For two cutoff policies j, m acting on the same ranked list, with retrieved sets R_j(q), R_m(q), and a utility that is
additive over documents at fixed normaliser — precision-type utilities u_m(q) = (1/|R_m(q)|)Σ_{d∈R_m(q)} r(q,d), or
recall-type utilities with known nG(q) — the difference u_j(q) − u_m(q) is a function of the relevance labels of the
symmetric difference Δ_{jm}(q) = R_j(q) △ R_m(q) only. Hence (a) judge errors outside Δ_{jm}(q) do not affect
D̂ − D, so ρ is governed by the judge's error inside the band; (b) a human audit of the pair-level differences needs
labels only on Δ_{jm}(q), i.e. |Δ_{jm}(q)|/|pool(q)| of the judgments.

Proof. Documents in R_j ∩ R_m contribute equally to both utilities and cancel in the difference; documents outside
R_j ∪ R_m contribute to neither. ∎

Caveat (set-F1). For set-F1 the normaliser |R_m| + nG(q) requires nG(q), the total number of relevant documents in the
pool, which depends on labels outside the band. Band localisation then holds only conditionally on nG(q); this is why
§10 reports that recalibration (which needs nG) behaves differently from selection, and why a pair-level audit for
set-F1 must add a PPI-corrected estimate of nG(q).

## What remains unproved / approximate
- Time-uniform (anytime-valid) version: replace the Bonferroni-over-looks with a confidence sequence for the paired
  mean (e.g. the PPI confidence sequence of Kilian et al. 2025); the conditional-validity argument is unchanged.
- The ρ lower bound (adoption score) has no finite-sample guarantee; it is an efficiency device (see §13.2, §13.5).
